# Swiggy Instamart vs Your Own D2C Website

Founders often frame this as a choice: list on Swiggy Instamart, or build your own website and sell direct. In practice most consumer brands end up doing both. The real question is which to build first, how much to invest in each, and what job each channel does.

This guide compares the two honestly. Instamart does not publish its commercial terms, so where its numbers matter we tell you what to ask for rather than guessing. It is part of our [quick-commerce guide for Mumbai brands](/quick-commerce-agency-mumbai/).

## What each channel is good at

**Swiggy Instamart** gives you demand you did not have to create. A customer already in the app, already buying milk, sees your product and adds it to the basket. Swiggy runs it as part of its consumer platform ([Swiggy corporate](https://www.swiggy.com/corporate/)), and brands manage the relationship through a login-only Brand Portal at [partner.instamart.in](https://partner.instamart.in/).

**A D2C website** gives you a customer you own. You get the name, phone number and order history, you set every price and bundle, and you decide what the brand looks and sounds like. You also pay for every visitor.

Neither is better. One is a rented shelf in a busy street; the other is your own shop on a quiet one.

Prepare the same core documents you would for Blinkit or Zepto: GST registration ([GST portal](https://www.gst.gov.in/)), an FSSAI licence for food ([FoSCoS](https://foscos.fssai.gov.in/)), trademark or brand authorisation, GS1 barcodes ([GS1 India](https://www.gs1india.org/)) and labels compliant with packaged-commodity rules ([Legal Metrology portal](https://lm.doca.gov.in/)). Our [Blinkit onboarding checklist](/guides/blinkit-seller-onboarding-checklist/) lists them in detail; most of it transfers.

What to request from the Instamart category team before signing:

1. The model of sale: does Instamart or a partner buy stock from you, or do you sell through the platform and pay fees?
2. Every deduction: margin or commission, logistics, marketing, penalties.
3. Payment terms in days.
4. Who sets the retail price and how price changes are agreed.
5. Launch cities and dark-store coverage for your SKUs.
6. Data access: store-level sales, availability, search terms.

## Building a D2C website

The costs here are more transparent because you choose every vendor.

- **Store platform.** Shopify publishes Indian pricing at [shopify.com/in/pricing](https://www.shopify.com/in/pricing); WooCommerce is free software ([woocommerce.com](https://woocommerce.com/)) but needs hosting and maintenance.
- **Payment gateway.** Gateways publish their transaction pricing, for example [Razorpay pricing](https://razorpay.com/pricing/). [VERIFY current rates per payment method.]
- **Shipping.** Courier aggregators and couriers price by weight slab and zone. Get a rate card for your actual parcel size.
- **GST compliance.** You invoice the customer directly and pay output GST. If you sell only through your own site, the e-commerce-operator provisions that apply to marketplace sellers generally do not apply to you in the same way. - **Demand generation.** This is the real cost, and it is open-ended. Performance marketing, SEO, content and social all cost money or time.

## The margin comparison, done properly

Comparing a "commission" on Instamart with "zero commission" on your own site is misleading. Your site has its own per-order costs. Use this structure for both channels, per unit, ex-GST.

| Cost line | Instamart | D2C website |
|---|---|---|
| Platform fees | Commission/margin, logistics, other charges [VERIFY all] | Store subscription ÷ orders; apps |
| Payment cost | Usually inside platform settlement [VERIFY] | Gateway fee per transaction |
| Fulfilment | Platform-run; may be charged [VERIFY] | Pick, pack, courier, packaging |
| Customer acquisition | In-app ads (optional but usually needed) | Ads, influencer, content: almost always needed |
| Returns and RTO | Per agreement [VERIFY] | Returns and COD refusals, both at your cost |

The method for filling in the quick-commerce column is in [quick-commerce commissions and margins in India](/guides/quick-commerce-commissions-and-margins-india/). For the D2C column, the line that usually surprises founders is customer acquisition cost on a first order; the line that rescues the economics is repeat purchase, which only a D2C channel lets you drive directly by email, WhatsApp and SMS with the customer's consent.

## Which first? A framework

**Start with Instamart (or another quick-commerce app) if:**

- your product is an impulse, top-up or daily-use item at a price that survives flat delivery fees;
- you can supply stock reliably to platform warehouses;
- you do not yet have a marketing budget to drive traffic to a website.

**Start with D2C if:**

- your product needs explanation, education or reviews before people buy;
- your range is wide, or you sell bundles, subscriptions or customisation;
- your price point or pack size is too large for a quick-commerce basket;
- you want customer data from day one to inform product development.

**Run both if:**

- you already have a working D2C site and want reach in metros without growing ad spend;
- or you are on quick commerce and want a channel for repeat buyers and larger packs.

## Channel conflict and how to manage it

Running both creates three risks.

1. **Price parity.** If your website is meaningfully cheaper, quick-commerce category teams will notice, and customers will screenshot it. Differentiate on pack size and bundles instead of discounting the same SKU.
2. **Stock allocation.** Platform warehouses need replenishment on their schedule. Keep a safety stock reserved for quick commerce so a D2C sale does not cause a dark-store stock-out.
3. **Cannibalisation.** In our experience it is smaller than founders fear: quick-commerce customers are buying for the next hour, D2C customers for the next month. Track it anyway by watching D2C order volume in cities where you launch on Instamart.

## Frequently asked questions

### What commission does Swiggy Instamart charge?

Swiggy does not publish a rate. Terms are agreed per brand through the Instamart category team. Get every deduction in writing and model contribution per unit before signing.

### Can I get customer data from Instamart?

Not individual customer details. Expect aggregated reports. Ask the category team exactly what the Brand Portal shows before you sign. [VERIFY]

### Is a D2C website worth it if I'm already on quick commerce?

Usually yes, once you have repeat buyers to capture and products that do not fit a dark store. The website is where you build a customer list that no platform controls.

### Which is cheaper to start?

A basic D2C store is often cheaper to set up, but it can stay dormant without marketing spend. Quick commerce costs more to enter but comes with demand. The costs are stock, compliance, packaging and freight to the platform warehouse. Instamart has not charged our clients an onboarding or listing fee, which makes it the cheapest of the three big apps to enter in cash terms; Blinkit charges about ₹25,000 per variant (returned as ad credit) and Zepto about ₹30,000 per variant (not returned). The catch is that Instamart is curated, so you need a category manager to accept the product.

### Do I need different packaging for each?

Often. Quick-commerce packs must survive dark-store handling and read on a small thumbnail; D2C shipments need outer packaging that survives courier networks. See [quick-commerce packaging requirements](/guides/quick-commerce-packaging-requirements/).
