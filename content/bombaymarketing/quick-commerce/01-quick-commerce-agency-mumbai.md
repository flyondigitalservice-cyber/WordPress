Quick commerce is the fastest-growing shelf in Indian retail, and it behaves nothing like Amazon or Flipkart. The buyer is two streets from a dark store, the decision takes seconds, and your product either is in that store, priced right and scannable, or it does not exist. ## What quick commerce actually is, and how it differs from a marketplace

On a marketplace such as Amazon, a buyer anywhere in India searches a catalogue of millions of listings, and the order travels from a fulfilment centre or your own warehouse over one to five days. Range is practically unlimited and the listing is the product.

Quick commerce runs on dark stores: small, closed-to-public warehouses placed inside residential catchments, each holding a limited assortment and serving a radius of a few kilometres. Blinkit's Seller Hub describes the flow plainly: you send inventory to Blinkit's warehouses, and Blinkit replenishes it across its dark stores and handles storage, fulfilment and delivery ([Blinkit Seller Hub, How to sell](https://seller.blinkit.com/how-to-sell)).

Four practical differences follow from that.

1. **Shelf space is physical and finite.** A dark store carries a few thousand SKUs, not millions. A slow SKU costs the platform real space, so it gets delisted or you pay storage on it.
2. **Availability is the ranking factor that matters most.** A listing that is out of stock in a store is invisible to every buyer served by that store.
3. **You pay for holding stock, not only for selling it.** Blinkit lists a per-unit inwarding fee and a daily storage fee on unsold units alongside commission and fulfilment ([Blinkit fees and commissions](https://seller.blinkit.com/fees-commission)).
4. **Pack and price architecture are different.** Impulse and top-up missions favour smaller packs and price points that survive a flat fulfilment charge.

## Listing and catalogue requirements

Blinkit's published requirement for a listing is product name and details, images, videos and other media, pricing and taxation details, and a valid Unique Product Code or barcode. Without a barcode you can request an exception code through Help and Support, subject to approval ([Blinkit FAQ](https://seller.blinkit.com/faq)).

In practice a catalogue that passes first time has the following.

- **A barcode that belongs to you.** GS1 India issues GTINs registered to your company ([gs1india.org](https://www.gs1india.org/)). Recycled or bought-in-bulk barcodes are a common cause of catalogue conflicts.
- **Titles that match the pack.** Brand, product, variant and net quantity, written exactly as printed.
- **Images shot for a phone tile.** The buyer sees a thumbnail roughly the size of a postage stamp. The front of pack must be legible at that size.
- **Tax data that matches your invoices.** HSN code and GST rate per SKU, consistent with what you file on the [GST portal](https://www.gst.gov.in/).
- **Label declarations that match the listing.** MRP, net quantity, manufacturer details and, for food, the FSSAI licence number and date marking required under the labelling regulations ([FSSAI regulations](https://www.fssai.gov.in/cms/food-safety-and-standards-regulations.php)).
- **Brand ownership proof.** Blinkit allows multiple brands on one account "provided you have the required brand trademark and authorizations".

Packaging is where physical goods get rejected at the warehouse door, even after the listing is approved. ## Pricing and margin maths, with a worked example

Quick commerce has more cost lines than a marketplace. Blinkit's published structure has six: inwarding fee per unit, daily storage fee per unsold unit, fulfilment fee per delivered order, category commission per sale, customer return fee where the return is attributed to the seller, and inventory removal fee per unit. There is also a Product ID activation fee, which Blinkit says is credited to your ads wallet ([Blinkit fees and commissions](https://seller.blinkit.com/fees-commission)). Our clients have been charged about ₹25,000 per product on Blinkit and about ₹30,000 on Zepto and Instamart [CONFIRM]. See the listing-fee section above.

Blinkit publishes the structure but not the rates. So the method matters more than any single number.

**Worked example. Every rate below is an illustrative assumption, not a platform rate.** Replace each one with the figure from your own Seller Hub commissions calculator or term sheet.

| Line | Assumption (illustrative) | ₹ per unit |
|---|---|---|
| MRP | Printed on pack | 249.00 |
| Selling price to customer | Your choice | 236.00 |
| GST inside selling price | 18% | 36.00 |
| **Net revenue (ex-GST)** | | **200.00** |
| Cost of goods, landed | Assumed | 70.00 |
| Category commission | Assumed 15% of ex-GST price | 30.00 |
| Fulfilment fee | Assumed flat ₹20 | 20.00 |
| Inwarding fee | Assumed ₹3 per unit | 3.00 |
| Storage fee | Assumed ₹1 a day for 10 days | 10.00 |
| Freight to platform warehouse | Assumed | 5.00 |
| In-app advertising | Assumed 10% of net revenue | 20.00 |
| Returns and damage provision | Assumed 2% | 4.00 |
| **Total cost** | | **162.00** |
| **Contribution per unit** | | **38.00 (19%)** |

Three things this example teaches that hold regardless of the real rates.

- **Flat fees punish low prices.** Halve the selling price and the assumed ₹20 fulfilment fee doubles as a share of revenue. This is why ₹49 packs rarely work unless they sell as part of a basket.
- **Slow stock is a cost.** In this model, 30 days on the shelf instead of 10 takes contribution from ₹38 to ₹18. Sell-through speed is a margin lever.
- **The listing fee needs volume to pay back.** At ₹38 contribution a unit, earning back a ₹30,000 listing fee that is *not* credited to ads takes 790 units. A SKU selling five units a day takes about five months; a SKU selling one a day takes more than two years. On Blinkit, where the ₹25,000 comes back as ad credit, it prepays the assumed ₹20-a-unit advertising on your first 1,250 units, but you pay it before you sell one.
- **Tax deductions are timing, not cost, but they hit cash.** Platforms charge GST on their service fees, which a registered seller normally claims as input credit. TCS under GST and TDS on e-commerce payments are deducted from payouts and reclaimed later. ## Dark-store fulfilment versus brand-owned fulfilment

There are two ways to deliver in minutes.

**Platform dark stores.** You ship cartons to the platform's warehouse against an appointment; the platform distributes to dark stores, picks, packs and delivers. You get demand and delivery on day one. You give up the customer relationship, pay the fees above, and plan stock by city.

**Brand-owned fulfilment.** You hold stock in your own or a third-party warehouse and deliver through your website, using courier or a hyperlocal partner. You keep customer data and full margin structure, but you fund demand generation and you cannot match ten-minute delivery outside a small radius.

Most Mumbai brands we work with run both: platform dark stores for reach and trial, own fulfilment for repeat orders, bundles and larger packs. The decision framework, with a cost comparison table, is in [dark store vs own warehouse](/guides/dark-store-vs-own-warehouse/).

## Common rejection reasons

These are the patterns we see repeatedly. They are observations from client work, not a published platform list.

1. **Name mismatch across documents.** The legal name on the GST certificate, bank account and FSSAI licence must be identical. Check your GST record on the [taxpayer search](https://services.gst.gov.in/services/searchtp) before applying.
2. **FSSAI licence that does not cover the activity or product category.** A licence issued for trading does not cover manufacturing. Verify on [FoSCoS](https://foscos.fssai.gov.in/).
3. **No trademark or authorisation.** Resellers without a brand authorisation letter stall at brand verification.
4. **Missing or duplicate barcodes.**
5. **Label non-compliance.** Missing MRP, net quantity, manufacturer address or date marking under the Legal Metrology packaged commodity rules ([Legal Metrology portal](https://lm.doca.gov.in/)).
6. **Insufficient shelf life at inwarding.** Platforms reject stock too close to expiry. The exact threshold is category-specific. [VERIFY in your inwarding guidelines.]
7. **Weak commercial case.** For curated platforms, a category manager needs evidence the SKU will sell: existing demand, a price gap, or a category the store lacks.
8. **Transit damage at the warehouse.** Leaking or crushed units fail quality checks and get returned at your cost.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need GST registration to sell on quick commerce?

Blinkit lists a GST certificate among its required documents, and states you can sell in 300+ cities "with a single GST and bank account". Zepto and Instamart do not publish their lists, but in our experience all three ask for GSTIN. Registration is done on the [GST portal](https://www.gst.gov.in/).

### Is there a fee to register?

There is no fee to register your business, but there is a fee for every product you put live. Blinkit calls it a Product ID activation fee and says it is credited to your ads wallet; our clients have paid about ₹25,000 per product. On Zepto and Instamart our clients have paid about ₹30,000 per product, and neither platform publishes whether it comes back as ad credit. [CONFIRM current amounts with each platform before activating.] For three SKUs on all three apps, budget about ₹2.55 lakh up front.

### What commission will I pay?

It depends on platform, category and price, and none of the three publishes a rate card. Use the Blinkit commissions calculator after login, and get Zepto and Instamart terms in writing. Our [commissions and margins guide](/guides/quick-commerce-commissions-and-margins-india/) shows how to model it.

### How long until my product is live?

Blinkit says as little as 7 days from registration. Plan for longer: listings, barcode issues, appointment slots and inwarding all add time. For Zepto and Instamart, ask the category team. [CONFIRM]

### When do I get paid?

Blinkit publishes two payout cycles a month, the 1st to 15th and the 16th to month end, processed on the third working day after each cycle, net of fees. Other platforms' terms sit in your agreement.

### Do I need FSSAI?

Only if you sell food, beverages or supplements regulated as food. Blinkit lists it as required "where applicable". Our [onboarding checklist](/guides/blinkit-seller-onboarding-checklist/) explains which licence type applies.

### Should I start with Blinkit or Zepto?

Usually the one whose model suits your stage. Blinkit is self-serve; Zepto is relationship-led. The trade-offs are in [Zepto vs Blinkit for your brand](/guides/zepto-vs-blinkit-for-your-brand/).

### Can I sell on quick commerce without changing my packaging?

Sometimes. If your pack has a scannable registered barcode, compliant declarations and survives being dropped in a rider's bag, yes. Otherwise read the [packaging requirements guide](/guides/quick-commerce-packaging-requirements/) first.
