Buying your first flat in Mumbai is less about finding the cheapest price per square foot and more about knowing your real budget, picking the right micro-market and avoiding the paperwork traps that cost buyers years. This guide walks you through all three, with a worked example and a calculator you can use for any flat.

Step 1: Work out your real budget (not just the flat price)

The number on the price sheet is only part of what you pay. For a typical resale or ready flat in Mumbai, plan for:

  • Down payment: banks usually lend 75–90% of the value, so keep at least 10–25% in cash.
  • Stamp duty: about 6% inside Mumbai (BMC) limits, about 7% in most other city limits such as Thane, Navi Mumbai and Pune. Women buyers get a 1% concession.
  • Registration: 1% of the value, capped at ₹30,000.
  • GST: 5% on under-construction flats (1% if the flat qualifies as affordable housing: carpet area up to 60 sq.m and value up to ₹45 lakh). No GST on ready flats with an Occupancy Certificate.
  • TDS: if the flat costs ₹50 lakh or more, you deduct 1% and deposit it with the tax department on the seller's behalf.
  • Other costs: lawyer's title search, society transfer and maintenance deposits, brokerage, shifting and basic interiors. Keep 2–4% aside.

Worked example: a ₹75 lakh flat in Mumbai

  • Down payment (20%): ₹15 lakh
  • Stamp duty (6%): ₹4.5 lakh, plus registration ₹30,000
  • Home loan: ₹60 lakh for 20 years at 8.5% means an EMI of about ₹52,000 a month
  • Cash needed on day one: roughly ₹20 lakh before interiors and brokerage

A simple rule banks also follow: keep your EMI under about 40% of your take-home pay. For a ₹52,000 EMI, that means a household take-home income of around ₹1.3 lakh a month. Try your own numbers below.

Step 2: Choose the right micro-market

"Budget" means different things across the Mumbai Metropolitan Region. As a rule, every step further from the island city along a rail line buys you more space for the same money. The trade-off is the commute.

Inside Mumbai city limits

  • Western suburbs: Malad, Kandivali, Borivali, Dahisar. Good rail and metro access and established social infrastructure. Budget buyers usually look at 1 BHK resale flats in older societies, or compact new flats in redevelopment projects.
  • Central and Harbour line: Chembur, Ghatkopar, Bhandup, Mulund. Older buildings at relatively lower rates, many going into redevelopment. Check the redevelopment status before you buy (see Step 3).

Just outside the city: more space for the money

  • Mira Road and Bhayandar: Western Railway access, with Metro Line 9 from Dahisar adding a second link.
  • Thane (Ghodbunder Road, Kalwa, Kolshet): large townships and good schools.
  • Kalyan, Dombivli, Badlapur, Ambernath: some of the most affordable new-construction flats in the MMR, with long but direct Central Railway commutes.
  • Navi Mumbai (Ulwe, Kharghar, Kamothe, Taloja, Panvel): planned CIDCO nodes that benefit from Atal Setu and the new Navi Mumbai International Airport. Read our Navi Mumbai budget flats guide.

Step 3: Ready, under-construction or resale?

Ready-to-move flats with an Occupancy Certificate (OC) carry no GST, no construction risk and you can move in or rent out at once. You pay more today, but you see exactly what you get.

Under-construction flats are usually cheaper and come with payment plans, but you pay GST and take a delay risk. Only buy MahaRERA-registered projects, and check the developer's track record of delivering on time.

Resale flats in older buildings are often the best value per square foot. They come with three extra checks:

  • Is the building in redevelopment, or about to be? That can mean a bigger new flat later, but also years of temporary housing.
  • Does the building have an OC and a Conveyance Deed in the society's name? Banks may refuse loans without them.
  • Are society dues, property tax and electricity bills paid up to date? Ask for a society No-Objection Certificate.

Step 4: The paperwork checklist

  1. MahaRERA registration for new projects: check the registration number, approved plans, carpet areas and possession date on the MahaRERA website.
  2. Carpet area in the agreement: under RERA, prices must be based on carpet area, not "super built-up" area.
  3. Title search by a lawyer, ideally covering 30 years, plus an encumbrance check for existing loans.
  4. Approvals: Commencement Certificate (CC) for new projects, OC for ready ones.
  5. Chain of agreements for resale flats: every earlier sale agreement, registered and stamped.
  6. Bank loan pre-approval before you pay a booking amount. Banks run their own legal check, which is a useful second opinion.

Step 5: Negotiate and close

Ask for the full cost sheet (base price, floor rise, parking, club and legal charges) before negotiating. Compare at least three similar flats in the same micro-market. For resale, a price backed by recent registered transactions in the building is the strongest negotiating tool. Stamp duty is calculated on the higher of the agreement value and the government ready-reckoner rate, so check that rate too.

FAQs

What is a realistic budget for a 1 BHK in Mumbai?

It depends heavily on the suburb and the building's age. Prices in island-city areas are many times those in the extended suburbs and MMR towns. Start from your EMI comfort level using the calculator above, then let us show you what that budget buys in each micro-market.

How much stamp duty do I pay on a flat in Mumbai?

About 6% of the property value inside Mumbai (BMC) limits, with a 1% concession for women buyers, plus 1% registration capped at ₹30,000. It is charged on the higher of the agreement value and the ready-reckoner rate.

Is GST payable on a resale flat?

No. GST applies only to under-construction property bought from a developer. Ready flats with an Occupancy Certificate and resale flats carry no GST.

How much home loan can I get?

Banks typically lend up to 75–90% of the property value, depending on the loan amount, and limit your EMIs to roughly 40–50% of your income. Your credit score, age and existing loans also matter.

Should I buy under-construction or ready to move?

Ready flats cost more but have no GST and no delivery risk. Under-construction flats are cheaper and offer payment plans, but buy only MahaRERA-registered projects from developers with a good delivery record.
