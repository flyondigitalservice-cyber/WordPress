Ask any first-time buyer in Mumbai what surprised them most and you will hear the same answer: the money needed on top of the flat price. This guide lists every charge you will meet when buying a home in Maharashtra, what it is based on, and how to plan for it.

1. Stamp duty

Stamp duty is a state tax paid when the sale agreement or deed is registered. In Maharashtra it is roughly:

  • Mumbai (BMC limits): about 6% of the property value.
  • Other municipal corporations such as Thane, Navi Mumbai and Pune: about 7%.
  • Smaller towns and rural areas (municipal councils and gram panchayats such as parts of Karjat, Khopoli and Alibaug): rates vary, so check the local rate.
  • Women buyers: a 1% concession, subject to conditions.

Stamp duty is charged on the higher of the agreement value and the government ready-reckoner rate for that location. You can calculate it on the IGR Maharashtra website.

2. Registration fee

1% of the property value, capped at ₹30,000 for residential property. It is paid along with stamp duty when the agreement is registered at the sub-registrar's office.

3. GST (only on under-construction property)

  • 5% on under-construction flats bought from a developer (without input tax credit).
  • 1% on affordable housing: carpet area up to 60 sq.m in metro areas (including the Mumbai region) and value up to ₹45 lakh.
  • No GST on ready flats that have an Occupancy Certificate, or on resale flats.

4. TDS on property purchase

If the property costs ₹50 lakh or more, the buyer must deduct 1% TDS from the payment to the seller and deposit it with the income tax department (Form 26QB). The seller must have a PAN; for NRI sellers, different and higher TDS rules apply.

5. Home loan costs

  • Processing fee, legal and valuation charges.
  • Stamp duty on the loan agreement or mortgage deed.
  • Optional home loan insurance (you are not obliged to buy the bank's policy).

6. Developer and society charges

  • Car parking, floor rise, preferred location charges (PLC).
  • Club membership, development and infrastructure charges.
  • Maintenance deposit (often 12–24 months in advance) and society formation charges.
  • For resale: society transfer fee and NOC.

Always ask for an all-inclusive cost sheet before you compare projects.

Quick estimate: what a ₹1 crore flat really costs

  • Stamp duty: ₹6–7 lakh, depending on location
  • Registration: ₹30,000
  • GST if under construction: ₹5 lakh
  • Loan, legal, society and other charges: typically ₹1–3 lakh

So an under-construction ₹1 crore flat can need around ₹12–15 lakh over the agreement value, before interiors.

RERA: the checks that protect you

Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, most projects with more than 8 units or more than 500 sq.m of land must be registered with MahaRERA before they are advertised. Before paying a booking amount:

  1. Look up the project's MahaRERA registration number and check approved plans, carpet areas and the promised completion date.
  2. Make sure the price is based on carpet area.
  3. Check the developer's quarterly progress updates and any complaints.
  4. Read the agreement for sale: possession date, compensation for delay and defect liability (5 years for structural defects under RERA).
  5. Never pay more than 10% of the cost before a registered agreement for sale is signed.

FAQs

What is the stamp duty on property in Mumbai?

About 6% of the property value inside Mumbai (BMC) limits, with a 1% concession for women buyers, plus 1% registration capped at ₹30,000.

What is the stamp duty in Navi Mumbai, Thane and Pune?

About 7% in these municipal corporation areas (about 6% for women buyers), plus registration. Check the exact rate for the location on the IGR Maharashtra website.

Is GST charged on a ready-to-move flat?

No. GST applies only to under-construction property bought from a developer. Ready flats with an Occupancy Certificate and resale flats do not attract GST.

Who pays TDS on a property purchase?

The buyer deducts 1% TDS when the property value is ₹50 lakh or more, and deposits it using Form 26QB. Different rules apply if the seller is an NRI.

How do I check if a project is RERA registered?

Search the project name or registration number on the MahaRERA website. The page shows approvals, carpet areas, the promised completion date and quarterly progress.
