# Bombay Sketchbook — a 12-reel drawing campaign for Bombay Marketing

Style: Urban Sketch · Pen & Wash (sepia fineliner drawn stroke by stroke, loose watercolour arriving after the line,
cream cold-press paper, handwriting in the margins). Format: 1080×1920 vertical, 24 fps, 30–40 s each, Hinglish voice,
handwritten roman-Hinglish captions, −14 LUFS. No third-party logos are drawn: marketplace names appear only as handwriting.
All claims come from bombaymarketing.in (see FACTS.md). No invented numbers.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **One tall sketchbook page per reel, the camera travels down it.** The pen draws a Mumbai moment as the hook, then
   each service deliverable is sketched and coloured in its own patch of the page, and the camera pulls back to the whole
   page, which is signed with the call to action.
2. **A page turn per deliverable.** Five small pages per reel. Clear, but the turns eat seconds and nothing accumulates.
3. **One street corner drawn through a day.** Beautiful, but it describes a place, not eleven different services.

**Chosen: 1.** A service is a list that has to add up; a page that fills and is then seen whole *is* that sum. The pull-back
(the page as frame) is the signature shot and the same in all 12, so the series is recognisable from one frame.

## 2. Logline and arc (the series)

A Mumbai seller's sketchbook: on every page a small shop gets one more thing it needs to sell online, until the last
page shows the whole road from free audit to growth.

Each reel: **setup** — a seller's problem, asked aloud while the pen draws it (0–4 s); **turn** — "Bombay Marketing
karega": the deliverables are drawn one by one and colour arrives in each; **ending** — pull back, the page is whole,
the pen signs the CTA: Free audit · bombaymarketing.in · WhatsApp +91 87676 97389.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Urban-sketching reportage pages** — learn: line first, wash second and off-register; margins kept for notes; the page
  as an object. Not taken: any artist's drawings or layouts.
- **Indian "chai-tapri explainer" reels (creator-economy finance/business explainers)** — learn: a question in the first
  two seconds, one idea per breath, warm second-person Hinglish, the CTA said once. Not taken: anyone's script, face, music.

## 4. Shot list (the grammar every reel uses; content differs)

| Shot | Framing / move | Dur | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Hook | Close on the top of the page; hold while the pen draws; slight push-in | 3–5 s | the act of noticing; question lands before colour |
| B Deliverables ×4–5 | Medium on each patch; camera cranes **down** the page on a spring, led by the pen; wash blooms after the last line | 4–6 s each | distance down the page = progress of the job |
| C Detail | Punch-in on the one coloured hero object of the reel | inside B | the thing the seller actually gets |
| D Whole page | Pull back to the full page, slight roll settling to 0 | 3 s | the sum; the sketch as an object |
| E Sign-off | Tilt into the lower margin; pen writes the CTA, red studio stamp presses | 5 s | from picture to words |

Camera moves used: hold-while-drawing, push-in, crane down on spring, punch-in, pull-back, tilt to margin.
Native move at the peak: **colour as arrival** — the page stays ink-only until "Bombay Marketing" is first spoken; then
wash blooms through everything already drawn.

## 5. Beat sheet (typical 34 s reel)

0.0 blank page, corner note "Mumbai · <place>" writes on · 0.3 pen starts hook sketch, hook line spoken · 3.5 near-silence
(pen lifts) · 4.0 "Bombay Marketing…" music enters, colour blooms · 4–26 deliverables, one per spoken line, each: pen draws
(~55 % of the line), wash (~25 %), label handwritten · 26 silence half a bar, pull back · 27.5 CTA line spoken, pen
writes URL and WhatsApp, stamp thump on the last beat · +1.2 s hold.

Picture is locked to the voice: each beat lasts its spoken line + 0.55 s; cuts and blooms are derived, not typed.

## 6. Cue map

Tempo 92–104 bpm depending on reel (three score variants A/B/C rotate; keys D, G, A). Bars are laid from the first
"Bombay Marketing". Section 1 (hook): no music, paper room tone + pen. Section 2 (deliverables): plucked string ostinato +
hand drum (tabla-like dayan/bayan synth) + bansuri-like flute answering between spoken lines. Section 3 (pull back):
drum drops out, held drone. Section 4 (CTA): full pattern returns, ends on a stamp + a single bell.

## 7. Sound design

| Section | Ambience | Foley | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | paper room tone; distant Mumbai street J-cut in (traffic hum, a train horn far off) | pen strokes per stroke | none |
| Bloom | street bed up | wet brush "shhh" | pluck + drone enter |
| Deliverables | street bed low | pen per stroke, brush per wash, soft tick on each label | full groove, ducked under voice |
| Pull back | near-silence (bed dips −12 dB) | page whoosh | drone only |
| CTA | bed | pen writing, stamp thump, bell | groove returns, final chord |

Two silences: before the bloom, before the pull-back.

## 8. Titles and captions

All text is handwriting on the page (Caveat for titles/labels, Reenie Beanie for the corner note). Captions: one
handwritten roman-Hinglish line in a caption strip in the lower margin of the frame, written on with the voice, held for
the whole line. The CTA is written into the page itself. Studio stamp: a red ink rubber-stamp "BOMBAY MARKETING ·
BUILD · LAUNCH · GROW" (a drawn stand-in, not the studio's real logo).
